Selected work

Closing a hidden 15–20% budget gap

Problem
Program plans budgeted consultants and training but left out flights, car rentals and per diems. Teams were under-budgeted, then overspent on costs they could not avoid.
Action
Introduced a standard planning template that makes teams flag and estimate these costs up front, so they are built into the budget from day one.
Result
Actual spend moved much closer to plan, and funds for other locations were protected.
Gap estimated at 15–20% of total program cost

Catching a partner’s mismatched expense report

Problem
An implementing partner’s receipts and supporting documents did not match the amounts reported or the funds received.
Action
Compared the approved budget to reported spend line by line, built an approved-versus-actual variance analysis and shared it with the partner.
Result
The partner corrected the errors, and the file went to the country office for final retirement with clean documentation.

Closing every open purchase order before the final report

Problem
Near the end of a project, a large number of purchase orders were still open, with contracts and implementation agreements running and some past their deadlines. All open purchase orders had to be closed before final reporting. It was not formally my responsibility.
Action
Built an offline dashboard of every open PO, loaded from the Excel tracker and kept offline to protect the data. Created with AI assistance and iterative prompting, it showed delivery status, amounts paid and amounts awaiting retirement. Worked with program officers to understand each implementing partner’s blockers and help with retirement documents. Presented the dashboard and weekly closure commitments to the global project manager, and put new PO requests on hold to protect the deadline.
Result
Open POs were cut by about half within two weeks and all were closed in roughly eight weeks, on time. The team was reported at a global meeting as the first in the organisation to do it.
Open POs halved in 2 weeks, all closed in about 8

Turning a contract review into a reusable playbook

Problem
A contract with a photojournalism group, hired to document beneficiary stories, hit friction partway through: expectations were not fully captured in the contract and information moved through several channels.
Action
Kept engaging throughout with regular stand-ups. When the contract ended, ran a structured review with the team on what worked, what did not and what to improve, covering both sides. Documented everything.
Result
A written lessons-learned guide for both parties. Two takeaways: define the terms of reference precisely, including how many events are covered and exactly what deliverable is expected, and name one single point of contact between the project team and the vendor so information is not duplicated or lost.

Documenting how the organisation actually works

Problem
The organisation needed a shared picture of its workflows, approval points and bottlenecks, and a go-to reference for new hires.
Action
Nominated to a committee set up to map every process, then selected as its secretary. The committee met and documented each process, covering required paperwork, approval points and bottlenecks, with workarounds identified. Coverage spanned HR (about five processes, including recruitment and onboarding), finance (about six), the program unit and the director’s office.
Result
Documented processes that give current staff a shared view of the workflow and new hires a reference for how things are done.
